# Don't Make Me Think (Krug, 2000)

## Citation
- Krug, S. (2000). *Don't Make Me Think: A Common Sense Approach to Web Usability*. New Riders. (Later revised editions: 2005, 2014 "Revisited".)

## Core idea
A good interface should be as **self-evident** (or at least self-explanatory) as possible. Users should not have to pause to figure out what something is, whether it is clickable, or what to do next. Every question mark a user has adds to cognitive effort and distracts from their goal.

## Key principles
- **Users scan, they don't read**: people skim pages for words/phrases that match their task or interest rather than reading thoroughly.
- **Users satisfice**: instead of evaluating all options to find the best one, users typically choose the first reasonable option that seems likely to work.
- **Users muddle through**: people often do not figure out how things work in detail; they use what works and move on.

## UX implications (distinct to this principle)
- **Make it obvious**: purpose, navigation, and primary actions should be immediately understandable without explanation.
- **Create clear visual hierarchy**: use prominence, grouping, and nesting to show what is important, what is related, and what belongs to what.
- **Use conventions**: follow established platform and web conventions so users can rely on prior experience (where things are, how they behave, what icons mean).
- **Make clickability/tappability clear**: interactive elements should look interactive; avoid ambiguity between text, labels, and controls.
- **Write for scanning**: short text, clear headings, meaningful labels, and front-loaded keywords; omit needless words.
- **Support satisficing**: make the "right" next step the most obvious, reasonable choice.

## Actionable checks
- [ ] Can a first-time user tell what this screen is for and what to do next within a few seconds?
- [ ] Is there a clear visual hierarchy that distinguishes primary content/action from secondary elements?
- [ ] Are labels and controls self-explanatory (no internal jargon, no clever-but-unclear phrasing)?
- [ ] Are common conventions followed for navigation, icons, and interactions?
- [ ] Is text scannable (concise headings/labels, short blocks, obvious keywords)?
- [ ] Is the most likely correct option easy to spot immediately?
